# UNSPENT: the vision

What the finished game is. `ROADMAP.md` says where it stands and what is next;
`LOOK.md`, `STORY.md` and `LANDSCAPES.md` hold the detail.

## Premise

- The few humans left after the machine apocalypse, hunted by half-broken
  machines. You are Elias Marr, a man out of time who helped cause it (`STORY.md`).
- The machines still mean to end humankind, but **many are indifferent**, as long
  as nobody interferes with their **ultimate plan**. The plan is the core arc;
  around it grow **generative subarcs**, finished in many different ways.

## Three factions

- **The machines**: each has a role in the plan and a disposition; interfere and
  they turn.
- **The alien intelligence and its craft**: a "higher" power the machines bargain
  with. To both, humans are "ants".
- **Humans who side with the machines.**

## The plan

- Visible from far off and learnt over the game: a tether rising to orbit, fields
  of servers drinking rivers, the land being remade.
- It advances in stages, region by region. The player can slow, stall, redirect,
  understand, or even help it.
- The machines are **barely functioning**; their failures are the player's tools.
- Interference, per region, rises with sabotage and theft and brings hunters.

## Realms

- **Surface**: oceans between continents; rarer landscapes belong to one continent.
- **Underground**: caves, the machines' mines, the buried city under the metropolis.
- **Orbital**: the tether station, the orbital foundry, a dead satellite ring.
- **Time**: the Before (this same coast, before the machines had the world) and
  the After (the plan finished). What you do in the Before changes the present.
- Joined by roads, shafts, portals and crafts. **A portal is a place, never a
  menu**; time is a mechanic, never a cutscene.

## Landscapes

- **At least 20 types, always procedurally composed.** Each seed deals its own
  world from them; no two maps match, every world is recognisably the same game.
- Within a realm they blend seamlessly at their borders.
- Owner's ideas still to come: floating land over a crater field, a shallow sea
  of rogue waves, an alien stronghold, foggy crags haunted by a force unknown even
  to the aliens. None may be faked with the wrong tool.

### What every landscape must have

- **Plan**: what the machines are doing here, and its works and depot.
- **Land**: its own ground, weather, light, hazards, sound and landmarks.
- **People**: who still lives here and how.
- **Player**: what there is to take, make and do.
- **A keeper**: a mecha sentinel, unique per type and "powerful differently".
  Beaten by tactics, never by trading hits; tells in its body, never text. Its
  fall changes the land for good. **To own a landscape, take it from its keeper.**

### A landscape is a chapter

> "Each landscape area is supposed to be far far far more immense and detailed
> with sub arcs and story paths and each one must be explored, mined, defended
> to advance to the next landscape and to progress in the game." (owner)

- The unit is a **region**: one connected run of one type. Each is its own chapter.
- A chapter asks three things: **explored**, **mined**, **defended** (keeper
  taken or depot put dark). Things the player was doing anyway, counted, never a
  checklist on the glass.
- The way on stays shut until then, and **shut is always diegetic**: a manned
  checkpoint, a dead lift, a bridge down. Never an invisible wall.
- Each region raises its own subarcs from its own state.
- Regions must be immense. "Big enough" is not the answer.

## Crafts

- Built or found; each opens new ground: raft, glider, hover sled, walker rig,
  drill crawler (down), submersible, the tether climber (up).

## Gear and mended tech

- Three idioms, which are also the tiers: **made** (by hand), **mended** (machine
  parts bound with human cord, most of the high tech you carry), **found**
  (machine tech taken whole: strongest, cannot be mended).
- Modular gear: slots take modules that grant resistances and abilities. The
  world asks, the gear answers, and loadouts are chosen for where you are going.
- Every weapon, armour and power is obtainable, **varied by region**, by rarity
  (dropped) or difficulty (crafted). A reward for going somewhere or beating
  something, never for playing long.
- A higher grade buys more modifiers, never a bigger number. Elite materials come
  from one landscape, one enemy or one keeper.
- Modifiers are "ultra high-tech refuse of all types": each changes a decision.

## Settlements and raids

- **Building is crafting at world scale**: shelter, holding, town, with defence
  and sustainability (power, food, water, heat) run by people who produce while
  you are away.
- Everything built can be taken away. **No raid timer, ever.** A holding is read
  by what it gives off (light, smoke, power, noise, stolen tech); a machine files
  it and carries the record home, and that encounter is playable.
- Attention builds into survey, probe, raid, siege, each warned by the world first
  and each answerable: fortify, hide, spoof, pay, leave.
- Taking the region's keeper quiets it for good.

## Non-negotiables

- **Nothing may look like Minecraft or any voxel game.** No visible grid, no cube
  terrain, no box props.
- **Every landscape hauntingly beautiful and specific**: detailed at walking
  scale, composed at distance, haunted by what was lost. "Beauty that aches is
  the bar, not pretty."
- **Beautifully dystopian** in every element, never a filter; neon and rain are
  accents that mean something.
- **The world is lit, not drawn** (`LOOK.md`), and each realm and era must be
  unmistakable at a glance.
- **The UI is one hacked slate**: a stolen machine display in a patched bezel.
  Every screen is an app on it.
- **Sound**: haunting, synth-heavy ambient, seamless across borders.
- Everything built in code; every world from its seed.
